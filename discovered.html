Three months in, the shedding starts

Three months after my client Jessica started Mounjaro, the shedding began. By month four, her shower drain was clogging every other day. By month six, her ponytail had lost half its volume.

She did everything everyone told her to do. She bought Nutrafol — $89 a bottle. She doubled up on biotin gummies — another $40. She added marine collagen powder to her morning coffee — $35 a tub. She forced down a special protein shake every afternoon — $47 for the bag.

Four months later, she'd spent over $400 on supplements alone. And her hair was getting worse.

"I was watching myself disappear in the mirror. The weight was finally coming off — and so was my hair. Nobody warned me about week 12." — Jessica, age 41 · 6 months into Mounjaro

By the time Jessica came to me, she was ready to quit her medication entirely. She'd lost 38 pounds. Her A1C was the lowest it had been in eight years. Her doctor was thrilled. But every morning she stood in front of the mirror and saw a woman she didn't recognize — thinner face, hollow cheeks, and a part line that kept getting wider.

I hear this story almost every week.

Why nothing she tried worked

Most hair vitamins on the market today were designed for one specific kind of woman: a postpartum mom, three to six months after childbirth, whose hair shedding is hormonal and temporary.

Those formulas assume your gut is absorbing nutrients normally. They assume you're eating roughly normal amounts of food. They assume your stomach empties on a normal schedule.

GLP-1 medications change all of that.

If you're on Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or any other semaglutide or tirzepatide formulation, three things are happening inside your body that no postpartum hair vitamin was designed to address:

What GLP-1s actually do to absorption

One: Your stomach empties up to 70% slower. Pills sit in your gut longer, often passing through without being broken down properly.

Two: Your appetite drops 40-60%. You eat less food, which means fewer absorption opportunities for the nutrients your follicles need.

Three: The bacteria in your gut shift. Iron, biotin, zinc, and B12 — the exact minerals critical for hair growth — depend on specific gut bacteria to be absorbed efficiently.

So Jessica's $400 worth of supplements? A significant portion of it went straight through her. Her body wasn't absorbing it. Her follicles never saw it.

This is why you can take biotin every single day for six months and still watch your hair fall out. It's not that biotin doesn't work. It's that you can't absorb enough of it while you're on a GLP-1.

The two-problem framework most women miss

Here's what makes GLP-1 hair loss particularly tricky to solve: there are actually two separate things happening to your hair at the same time. Most products only address one.

Problem one: telogen effluvium

Rapid weight loss is a stressor. Your body interprets it as a crisis — even a beneficial one. In response, hair follicles shift from growth phase into resting phase, all at roughly the same time.

Two to three months after the metabolic shock begins, those resting follicles release their hairs. All at once. That's when you start finding clumps in the shower drain.

This is called telogen effluvium, and it's the same mechanism that causes postpartum hair loss. Standard hair vitamins are designed for this.

Problem two: nutrient malabsorption

While your follicles are stuck in resting phase, your body simultaneously needs more nutrients than usual to recover. But you're absorbing fewer nutrients because of the gut changes from your medication.

This is the part nobody talks about. And it's why standard hair vitamins designed for postpartum women fail on GLP-1 users.

If a hair supplement only addresses one of these problems, you'll keep losing hair while you're on the medication.

What you need is a formula designed specifically for the GLP-1 environment — one that uses bioavailable forms of the right nutrients, in the right combinations, that can be absorbed even when your gut is sluggish.
