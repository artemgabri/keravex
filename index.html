The biology of GLP-1 hair loss

The Three-Way Nutrient Blockade

GLP-1 medications don't damage your follicles. They cut off the supply chain. Three simultaneous absorption problems mean any hair vitamin that doesn't address all three is leaking out the bottom.

1

Caloric reduction

40-60%

GLP-1s cut appetite by nearly half. Cleveland Clinic: "rapid, restricted eating triggers telogen effluvium because the body diverts limited nutrients to vital organs first."

2

Slowed gastric emptying

70%

Semaglutide slows gastric emptying by up to 70%. Food sits longer with reduced acid output, dramatically changing how minerals like iron and zinc reach your bloodstream.

3

Microbiome disruption

50%

GLP-1s shift gut bacteria within weeks. Several of the bacteria that disappear are the ones synthesizing B-vitamins and biotin in your colon — eating "enough" still runs a deficit.

A letter from Dr. Matthews

"I prescribed semaglutide to 847 women before I started really listening to them."

I'm Dr. Lauren Matthews. I'm a board-certified endocrinologist with 22 years of clinical practice — most of them spent helping women navigate metabolic disease, PCOS, and the hormonal aftermath of weight loss.

When semaglutide and tirzepatide reshaped my practice in 2022, I prescribed them to anyone who qualified. The results were extraordinary. Patients dropping 40, 50, 80 pounds. Diabetics weaning off insulin.

But somewhere around patient 100, I started getting the same complaint at the four-month checkup: "My hair is falling out."

At first I did what most physicians do — I told them it was telogen effluvium, it would self-resolve, and they should focus on their metabolic gains. I prescribed biotin and a multivitamin. I told them to be patient.

Then a patient I'll call Margaret came in for her six-month visit. She had lost 67 pounds. Her A1C was 5.4. She'd been off blood pressure medication for two months. She also had visible scalp through the part on the top of her head. She was wearing a baseball cap to her job as a real estate broker.

She told me: "Dr. Matthews, I have my body back. But I look in the mirror and don't recognize my reflection. I would rather be heavier than feel this invisible."

That night I pulled the data on every GLP-1 patient in my practice. Roughly one in three women had developed visible hair loss within four to six months. The standard answer — be patient — was clinically correct. The lived experience of waiting 12 to 18 months was something I had completely failed to account for.

I spent 14 months reading every paper I could find on telogen effluvium, hair cycle biology, and the specific nutrient deficiencies that emerge under reduced caloric intake.

What I found changed how I think about GLP-1 hair loss entirely.

There is a window — roughly 90 to 120 days from the first visible shed — where follicles that have shifted into the resting phase are still receptive, still vascularized, still capable of restarting growth on a normal cycle. Inside the window, the right nutrient repletion compresses the full recovery from 12–18 months down to 3 to 4 months.

If you miss it, follicles begin shifting into chronic telogen effluvium, where shedding doesn't self-resolve and recovery takes a year or longer.

I call this The Recovery Window.

Iron (Bisglycinate)
18 mg

The most common driver of telogen effluvium in women. Bisglycinate is chelated to glycine — protects from stomach acid, absorbs 2-4× better than ferrous sulfate.

Why this form: doesn't cause the constipation cheap iron is famous for. Critical for GLP-1 users with reduced gastric acid. Pineda 2001 showed bisglycinate maintains absorption even when stomach pH is elevated — the exact condition GLP-1s create.
Anchor study: Pineda O., 2001 J Trace Elem Med Biol — "Effectiveness of iron amino acid chelate."
Zinc (Picolinate)
15 mg

Required for follicle cell division. Without it, the cells at the base of the follicle can't multiply. Picolinate has the highest absorption rate in research with reduced caloric intake.

Why this form: what clinical nutritionists choose; what most retail brands skip because it's 4× the cost of zinc oxide. Critical for the malabsorption profile of GLP-1 users.
Form rationale: AAD Position on TE Nutrient Deficiencies, 2024.
Methylated B-Complex
Full DV

Methylcobalamin (B12) + methylfolate + P-5-P (B6) — the active forms your body uses directly. The 30-40% of women with MTHFR genetic variants can't efficiently convert non-methylated forms.

Why this form: bypasses the genetic bottleneck that affects up to 40% of women. Particularly important for GLP-1 users since microbiome disruption further reduces endogenous B-vitamin synthesis.
Genetic prevalence: MTHFR C677T variant frequency, Wilcken et al. 2003.
Marine Collagen (Peptide-weight)
2,500 mg

Hair is keratin built from amino acids — proline, glycine, hydroxyproline. Marine collagen is hydrolyzed to peptide weight; doesn't require full protein digestion to absorb.

Why this form: matters specifically when GLP-1 protein intake is below RDA and digestion is too slow for whole proteins. Peptide weight (less than 5 kDa) crosses the gut wall intact.
Form rationale: published research on hydrolyzed collagen bioavailability.
Saw Palmetto Extract
320 mg

Rapid weight loss can transiently elevate DHT — the androgen that miniaturizes follicles in pattern hair loss. Saw palmetto is a natural 5-alpha reductase support.

Why this form: studied as a gentler alternative to finasteride — included for women noticing thinning at temples and crown. The 320mg dose matches published clinical research thresholds.
Form rationale: published studies on saw palmetto and androgenic alopecia.
Biotin
5,000 mcg

Yes, biotin. But not as the headline ingredient. Biotin is structurally necessary for keratin synthesis but on its own does almost nothing for GLP-1 hair loss.

Why this dose: clinically relevant supporting role, not the lead actor. Most hair vitamins fail because they're 90% biotin and 10% everything else. Keravex inverts that.
Note: high-dose biotin can interfere with thyroid lab tests — discontinue 48hr before bloodwork.
